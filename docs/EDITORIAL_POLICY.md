# Editorial and accessibility policy

Phoenix Scrolls is dedicated exclusively to the Herculaneum papyri and the research that makes them readable.

## Intended audience
Readers without training in ancient Greek, papyrology, or archaeology. Scholarly source links are always available but never required to begin reading.

## Three layers
1. **Greek**: an attributed scholarly transcription. Preserve lacunae, uncertain readings and restorations.
2. **Close English**: a source-attributed translation, or a separately credited original working translation. Do not silently rewrite published translations.
3. **Modern reading**: an interpretive adaptation in clear contemporary English, explicitly labeled as such. Do not invent missing premises or conflate uncertainty with certainty.

## Revision workflow
Every published passage needs a permanent passage ID, edition ID, date, source citation, editorial status and rights record. A new decipherment creates a new edition, retaining previous editions. Any dependent translation or modern reading must be flagged for review if its source reading changes.

## Review status
- placeholder: no actual text available
- draft: editorial work not independently checked
- source-verified: checked against a cited scholarly edition
- expert-reviewed: checked by a qualified Greek/papyrology reviewer

## Rights
Do not assume an ancient work being public domain means every modern transcription, critical edition, English translation or papyrus image is reusable. Keep a rights manifest per asset. CC BY-NC 4.0 has attribution, change-labeling and noncommercial requirements; the scholarly paper's arXiv distribution license is not itself an unrestricted republication grant.

## Reader UX
- Greek and close English are visible by default.
- Modern reading is optional, not substituted for the translation.
- Mobile layouts stack or switch panels without losing passage location.
- Avoid color-only uncertainty cues.
- Clearly distinguish interpretive context from words attributed to the ancient author.
